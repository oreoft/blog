## About Me

A programmer who graduated in 2020 and is currently in my odyssey phase, striving to become an outstanding developer.

I love programming, technology, and learning. I enjoy that feeling of using code to make the world obey my commands. Coding makes my logic more rigorous and my thinking more flexible. I like the design process from “zero” to “one,” and I also like carefully sculpting that “one” into “∞” like an artistic master.

I also love hardware, tinkering, and knowledge. I’m amazed by those tiny magical silicon blocks that represent the pinnacle of human wisdom, and I marvel at how rapidly evolving technology keeps bringing us endless possibilities. Browsing for “junk” on Xianyu is a hobby outside of work. I enjoy building PCs, Hackintosh, soft routers, and NAS setups. I’m extremely sensitive to performance specs—I may not need it, but you can’t not have it.

I also love life, myself, and the unknown. I admire the charm of classical Chinese poetry and the spirit of Su Dongpo. I long for poetry and distant horizons as well, walking along the journey of life. Life is short, so let me try new tea with a fresh fire. Enjoy poetry and wine while we’re still young.

## Why I Started This Blog

Building a blog is really something I had always wanted to do but never actually did, because I had benefited so much from other great bloggers’ sites since high school. So I made myself a promise: one day, I’d have my own blog too. Later, when I was in college, I never had the motivation to write, though the idea always stayed in my mind. Honestly, the main reason was that college was just too much fun.... Then preparing for the computer science graduate entrance exam took up a huge amount of time too (and annoyingly, I still didn’t get in, sob sob). Now that I’ve been working for a while, I’ve realized that a blog is actually very important for technical people. Old thoughts plus new thoughts together led me to write this about page—does this count as my first blog post?

Besides that original seed in my heart, I’ve summed up a few reasons for writing a blog. They’re also a reminder to myself that I hope I won’t waste this, that I can keep writing, and stay true to my original intention:

**1. Deepen memory and improve my ability to explain things clearly**

> I’m a perfectionist—or maybe a fake perfectionist... because I’m actually pretty messy. But back to the point: my “perfectionism” is about making myself feel comfortable with what I do. I have extremely strict standards for layout, formatting, the content of messages I send to others, even whether screenshots include an *Alpha* channel or not. Because of this, before writing a blog post, I spend more time figuring out how to express my thoughts. And before I can express them, I need to reorganize and review the content, understand it again, absorb it again, and sort out the logic in my head.
>
> *A lot of the time, you think you understand something—but when you try to explain it again or write it down with pen and paper, you suddenly don’t know where to start.*

**2. The spirit of sharing on the Internet**

> Through sharing, I can get direct and immediate returns. These returns aren’t material—they’re spiritual. I get really addicted to that kind of spiritual reward, nothing utilitarian about it, just like.

**3. Recording bits and pieces of growth**

> As I write this about page, I’ve just graduated this year, and I’m working and learning at a startup in Wuhan. I don’t know what the future will look like, but I don’t want to look back one day and find that I left behind no record at all.
>
> I think all of us have had similar experiences: after a long time, we suddenly look back at things we once did or said and feel they were incredibly stupid. There’s even the “visible for three days” feature on WeChat to save you from embarrassment. But it’s exactly those stupid years that help us keep growing, constantly helping us reposition ourselves and correct our mistakes along the way.
>
> By the way, suddenly a quarter of my life is almost gone. Sometimes it’s actually nice to look back at old records, even if I felt nothing at the time. It’s a bit of a shame that I didn’t leave behind more records in high school and college.

**4. Exploring more unknown worlds**

> The world is not limited to just my home, my company, or my social circle. I think the Internet is a parallel space, and through it I can experience a much bigger world. By writing a blog, I know there are many people out there just like me—reading blogs, writing blogs, existing alongside me. In this parallel space, these people and this knowledge are waiting for me in some corner of the world.

**5. Helping and giving back to people who want help** 

> I intentionally put this one last, and it’s also the thing I encounter most often at work—the source that motivates me every time to write something. As a newbie exploring unfamiliar areas of knowledge, I often run into walls. Sometimes I can’t even get the environment set up properly. When I’m lucky, I quickly come across a really good article that solves my difficulties and frustrations. But most of the time, I can’t find an article that explains things in exactly the way I need.
>
> If I come across an article that solves my problem perfectly, I’m grateful, and I want to write a blog post in return as a knowledge payback—to keep the technical relay going. If I *don’t* come across an article that solves my problem perfectly, but I manage to solve it myself, then I want to share that knowledge too—to fill that gap in case someone else, like me, also couldn’t find an article that solved the problem.
