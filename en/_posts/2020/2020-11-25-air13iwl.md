## Preface

The meaning of life really is all about tinkering, so let me first talk about why this blog post exists. I used to have a laptop, but after graduation, the place I rented was very close to the company, and the company provided a computer plus two monitors, which made both work and studying super convenient. With the mindset of happily freeloading on the company setup, I got used to spending most of my time there. So the laptop sitting at home started to feel pretty useless, and keeping it around just meant letting it sit there depreciating. As a qualified junk-tech scavenger, how could I let that happen? So I sold it.

But—but—but—the New Year holiday was coming up soon, and since the weather was pretty cold, sometimes I just didn’t feel like staying at the office too late. After getting home, I still wanted to write some code, jot down some thoughts, and watch some online courses. Gradually, the need became more and more real, so I started thinking about buying another laptop. Since I wouldn’t be using it in that many scenarios and it was mostly just meant to fill in when needed, I didn’t set the budget very high. In the end, I settled on the Xiaoxin Air 13 IWL. Cheap, powerful, and perfectly suited my needs. And of course, after buying it, I had to mess around and turn it into a Hackintosh. I just didn’t expect it to work out this perfectly.

### BIOS Settings

To get a better macOS experience, you need to adjust some BIOS parameters so the hardware is more compatible with macOS. I’ll go through it step by step below. Note that here we need to enter the hidden BIOS settings, which contain some more advanced options, so it’s not just a simple matter of pressing F2.

1. Press F2 during boot to enter BIOS

2. Then shut down. While shutting down, press the keys in the direction shown by the arrow below. You don’t need to press them one by one—you can slide across them—but you need to do it quickly. If it doesn’t work, go back to step 1 and try again.

   ![image-20210404110421013](https://mypicgogo.oss-cn-hangzhou.aliyuncs.com/tuchuang202307171437729.webp)

<center>Start from F2 and slide across in one go</center>



![image-20210404110525677](https://mypicgogo.oss-cn-hangzhou.aliyuncs.com/tuchuang202307171437889.webp)

<center>If you see this debug screen, it means you succeeded</center>



3. Start configuring
   1. Configuration->System Performance Mode: `Intelligent`   // Fixes the issue where some drivers stop working when the battery gets low
     
   2. Security->Intel SGX: `Software Control` //  Lets software control the trusted execution area used for encryption
     
   3. Exit->OS Optimized Defaults: `Disabled` // Disable default optimization
     
   4. Advanced->Power & Performance->CPU - Power Management Control->CPU Lock Configuration->CFG Lock:`Disabled` // This one is strongly recommended to disable for OpenCore, so the OS can take over sleep control, power management, and CPU turbo control
     
   5. Advanced->System Agent (SA) Configuration->Graphics Configuration->DVMT Pre-Allocated:`64M`  // Dynamically allocates shared video memory; the more the better. This determines whether macOS will drop frames
     
   6. Boot->Fast Boot: `Disabled` // Disable Windows fast startup
